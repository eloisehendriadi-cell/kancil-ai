🌿 🧠 Topic Overview

  • The circulatory system in mammals consists of blood vessels, the heart, and valves to ensure one-way blood flow.
  • Blood is composed of plasma, red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets.
  • The heart functions as a pump, facilitating double circulation: pulmonary and systemic.
  • Arteries carry oxygenated blood away from the heart, while veins return deoxygenated blood.
  • Capillaries link arteries and veins, allowing for substance exchange with tissue cells.

🔑 Key Concepts

  • Blood: A fluid tissue that transports nutrients, gases, and waste.
  • Heart: The muscular organ that pumps blood through the circulatory system.
  • Arteries: Blood vessels with thick walls that carry blood away from the heart.
  • Veins: Blood vessels with thinner walls that return blood to the heart.
  • Capillaries: Microscopic vessels where gas and nutrient exchange occurs.
  • Pulse: The rhythmic expansion of arteries with each heartbeat.

🧬 Processes / Mechanisms

  1. Blood is pumped from the heart into arteries under high pressure.
  2. Arteries branch into smaller arterioles, leading to capillaries.
  3. In capillaries, oxygen and nutrients diffuse into tissue cells, while waste products enter the blood.
  4. Deoxygenated blood returns through venules to veins and back to the heart.

💡 Applications / Examples

  • The four-chambered heart separates oxygenated and deoxygenated blood, enhancing efficiency.
  • Coronary heart disease can result from atherosclerosis, leading to heart attacks.

✍️ Mini-Quiz (3 quick checks)

  • Q: What is the main function of capillaries?
  • Answer: To link arteries to veins and facilitate substance exchange.
  • Q: How does the structure of arteries relate to their function?
  • Answer: Thick muscular walls allow them to withstand high pressure.
  • Q: What is the role of valves in veins?
  • Answer: To prevent backflow of blood as it returns to the heart.
