🌿 📚 Study Notes on Child Marriage in Pakistan

  • Child marriage is a significant global issue, affecting millions of children, especially girls. Over 650 million women were married before age 18, with 12 million girls married annually before adulthood (UNICEF).
  • The practice is prevalent in South Asia, sub-Saharan Africa, and parts of the Middle East and Latin America, with Niger, Chad, and Bangladesh having the highest rates.
  • Pakistan's legal framework includes the Sindh Child Marriage Restriction Act of 2013, which raises the legal marriage age to 18 and imposes penalties for facilitating child marriages.
  • The Punjab Marriage Restriction (Amendment) Act of 2015 increased penalties for arranging marriages below the legal age, although the minimum age for girls remains 16.
  • Cultural norms, poverty, and gender inequality are major factors driving child marriage, often viewed as a means to alleviate financial strain or protect girls from perceived dangers.
  • Child marriage leads to severe long-term consequences, including increased dropout rates from school, early pregnancies, and domestic abuse, impacting social stability and economic growth.
  • Pakistan proposes a hybrid marriage registration system to improve local enforcement and accountability, combining digital and paper-based methods to monitor marriages effectively.
  • The Program for Lady Health Workers (LHWs) provides reproductive health education and services to young brides, helping to mitigate the health risks associated with early marriage.

🔑 Key Concepts

  • Child Marriage: Marriage before the age of 18, often leading to negative social and health outcomes.
  • Sindh Child Marriage Restriction Act: Law raising the legal marriage age to 18 in Sindh province.
  • Punjab Marriage Restriction (Amendment) Act: Law increasing penalties for underage marriages in Punjab.
  • Gender Inequality: Disparities in rights and opportunities based on gender, contributing to child marriage.
  • Cultural Norms: Societal beliefs that can perpetuate practices like child marriage.
  • Lady Health Workers (LHWs): Trained female health workers providing reproductive health services.

🧬 Processes / Mechanisms

  1. Identification of child marriage cases through community reporting.
  2. Legal intervention by courts and law enforcement based on reported cases.
  3. Implementation of educational and health programs by LHWs to support affected girls.
