🌿 Learning

🧠 Topic Overview

  • Direct proportion expresses a relationship where an increase in one quantity results in a proportional increase in another.
  • Inverse proportion indicates that an increase in one quantity leads to a proportional decrease in another.
  • The constant of proportionality links the two quantities in both direct and inverse proportions.
  • Real-life applications include scenarios like grocery pricing and workforce efficiency in construction.
  • Algebraic expressions can be used to find unknown quantities in both types of proportional relationships.

🔑 Key Concepts

  • Direct Proportion: A relationship where two quantities increase or decrease together at a constant rate.
  • Inverse Proportion: A relationship where an increase in one quantity results in a decrease in another, maintaining a constant product.
  • Constant of Proportionality: The constant multiplier that relates two directly proportional quantities.

🧬 Processes / Mechanisms

  1. Identify the quantities involved (e.g., rice cost and weight).
  2. Establish the relationship (direct or inverse).
  3. Use algebraic expressions to find unknown quantities based on known values.

💡 Applications / Examples

  • In a supermarket, 6 kg of rice costs $24, leading to a cost of $52 for 13 kg.
  • If 5 workers take 150 days to build a house, 10 workers can complete it in 75 days.

✍️ Mini-Quiz (3 quick checks)

  • Answer: y = kx.
  • Q: How does increasing the number of workers affect the time to build a house?
