🌿 🧠 Topic Overview

  • Food provides energy for cell activities and is essential for making new cytoplasm.
  • Water is a crucial solvent, helps regulate body temperature, and is necessary for photosynthesis.
  • Carbohydrates are organic molecules made of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen, with a 2:1 ratio of hydrogen to oxygen.
  • Fats are organic molecules composed of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen, with less oxygen relative to hydrogen.
  • Proteins are made from amino acids and contain carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, and sometimes sulfur.
  • Testing for reducing sugars involves using Benedict’s solution, while starch is tested with iodine solution.
  • The Biuret test is used to identify proteins, resulting in a violet color if proteins are present.

🔑 Key Concepts

  • Proteins: Organic molecules made from amino acids; essential for cell functions.
  • Fats: Organic molecules providing energy and insulation; made from fatty acids and glycerol.
  • Carbohydrates: Organic molecules serving as energy sources; includes sugars and starches.
  • Water: Vital solvent and temperature regulator in living organisms.
  • Benedict’s solution: A reagent used to test for reducing sugars.
  • Biuret reagent: A solution used to test for proteins.

🧬 Processes / Mechanisms

  1. Testing for Reducing Sugars:
    - Use Benedict’s solution.
    - Heat the mixture.
    - Observe color change (blue to green/orange/red).

  2. Testing for Starch:
    - Apply iodine solution to the sample.
    - Observe color change (yellow-brown to blue-black).

  3. Testing for Proteins:
    - Mix sample with Biuret reagent.
    - Observe color change (blue to violet).

💡 Applications / Examples

  • Foods rich in proteins include milk, eggs, and meat.
  • Foods rich in fats include butter, cheese, and nuts.
  • Carbohydrates are found in grains and starchy vegetables.
