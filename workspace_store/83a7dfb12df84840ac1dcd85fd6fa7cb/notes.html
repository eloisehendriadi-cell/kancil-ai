🌿 📚 Learning

  • Direct Proportion: When two quantities increase or decrease at the same rate, they are directly proportional, linked by a constant multiplier known as the Constant of Proportionality.
  • Inverse Proportion: When one quantity increases, the other decreases at a proportional rate, maintaining a constant product.
  • Algebraic Expressions: Direct proportion can be expressed as ( y \propto x ) or ( y = kx ), while inverse proportion is expressed as ( y \propto \frac{1}{x} ) or ( xy = k ).
  • Case Study 1: In a supermarket, 6 kg of rice costs $24, leading to a cost of $52 for 13 kg of rice.
  • Case Study 2: If 5 workers take 150 days to build 1 house, it would take 10 workers 75 days and 12 workers 62.5 days.
  • Finding Unknown Quantities: Algebraic expressions of direct and inverse proportions can be used to find unknown quantities in various scenarios.
  • Graphical Representation: Direct proportions yield straight-line graphs through the origin, while inverse proportions produce hyperbolic graphs.

🔑 Key Concepts

  • Direct Proportion: Relationship where one quantity increases/decreases with another at the same rate.
  • Inverse Proportion: Relationship where one quantity increases while the other decreases.
  • Constant of Proportionality: The constant multiplier linking two directly proportional quantities.

🧬 Processes / Mechanisms

  1. Identify the quantities involved.
  2. Determine if the relationship is direct or inverse.
  3. Use the appropriate algebraic expression to find unknown values.
